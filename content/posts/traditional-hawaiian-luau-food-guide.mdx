A luau is the traditional Hawaiian feast, held to celebrate births, weddings, graduations, and homecomings. The word originally referred to the taro leaves used in *lau lau* — leaf-wrapped bundles of fish steamed over hot stones. Over time it came to mean the whole event.

If you've attended a commercial luau at a resort hotel, what you experienced is a distant cousin of the real thing: a performance built around a caricature of the tradition. The food at a real Hawaiian luau is something else entirely — specific, simple, rooted in techniques developed over centuries.

Here's what's actually on the table.

## The core dishes

**Poi**
The centerpiece of the traditional Hawaiian table. Cooked taro root mashed with water, fermented for one to three days. Scooped with two fingers and eaten alongside salted fish, poke, or greens. The flavor ranges from mild and starchy (fresh) to tangy and sharp (fermented). [We've written a full guide to poi here.](/blog/what-is-poi-a-complete-guide-to-hawaiis-original-superfood)

**Lau lau**
Fish — traditionally butterfish or cod — and taro leaves wrapped together in ti leaves and steamed for several hours until the taro leaves have completely softened into a savory, silky bundle. The leaves behave almost like slow-cooked spinach; they absorb the fat from the fish and create something deeply satisfying.

To make lau lau at home, a [two-tier bamboo steamer](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=bamboo+steamer+10+inch&tag=mypacificnutr-20) gives you enough room for multiple bundles at once. Steam for at least 2 hours — the taro leaves need time to break down fully.

**Lomi salmon**
Diced salted salmon — or fresh salmon cured overnight in sea salt — massaged (*lomi* means "to massage") with diced tomato, green onion, and crushed ice. Served cold, as a condiment alongside poi. The salting technique arrived with 18th-century traders; the dish became traditionally Hawaiian within a generation.

**Poke**
Cubed raw ʻahi tuna dressed with [coconut aminos](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=coconut+secret+coconut+aminos&tag=mypacificnutr-20) or soy sauce, sesame oil, and [ʻAlaea Hawaiian sea salt](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=alaea+hawaiian+sea+salt&tag=mypacificnutr-20). The condiment at the modern luau; traditionally the main event, eaten with poi as the whole meal. [Full guide to poke here.](/blog/poke-bowl-history-and-how-to-make-it-at-home)

**Haupia**
Coconut milk set with arrowroot or cornstarch, served cold in squares. The dessert at every luau table — barely sweet, creamy, cooling. [Full guide to haupia here.](/blog/what-is-haupia-hawaiian-coconut-pudding)

**Steamed rice**
Short-grain white rice, the carbohydrate base alongside poi. Not traditional pre-contact, but fully integrated into the Hawaiian table by the early 20th century through the influence of Japanese, Chinese, and Filipino communities.

## How to build a luau menu at home

A full luau for four people is manageable with advance prep.

**The day before:**
- Salt the salmon for lomi salmon (coat in sea salt, refrigerate overnight, rinse before using)
- Make haupia and refrigerate to set

**Day of:**
- Start lau lau in the steamer 2.5 to 3 hours before the table
- Make fresh poke 30 minutes before serving
- Cook rice; thaw frozen poi if using
- Assemble lomi salmon with tomato, onion, and crushed ice just before serving

What you'll notice: this is less work than a Western dinner party of equivalent impressiveness. Pacific cooking is built around techniques — steaming, curing, raw preparation — that reward patience over complexity.

## A note on the imu

The traditional Hawaiian luau centered on the *imu* — an underground oven of heated volcanic rocks. Food wrapped in ti leaves was lowered in, covered with earth, and cooked for six to eight hours by retained steam heat. The result was incomparably tender and smoky.

You cannot replicate the imu in a home kitchen. What you can do is understand what it achieved — slow, moist heat, tenderizing through time — and make intelligent substitutions. A bamboo steamer or a slow cooker gets you most of the way there for most of the dishes.
